Who we are

Philanthropy done with communities, not to them.

Founded in 2021, the Foundation is funded by contributions from clean energy project revenue generated through Community Impact PPAs. We fund and support organizations that build healthy communities in rural and urban America.

With the guidance and contributions of our corporate partners, including Microsoft and Google, we provide essential financial and programmatic support to community-based organizations nationwide, and we prepare the next generation of energy and sustainability leaders through our HBCU fellowship.

A trusted partner investing
in healthy communities
in rural and urban America

Sharing the Power Foundation contributes to causes that provide renewable energy access in vulnerable urban and rural communities. From Appalachia to Atlanta, these organizations promote access to clean air, water and renewable energy; residential and commercial weatherization and energy efficiency; workforce training in clean energy careers; and effective solutions to expand the availability of affordable food and transportation.

Our mission

Turning energy infrastructure into lasting community benefit

Sharing the Power Foundation is a 501(c)(3) non-profit organization that provides funding and resources to organizations and programs that build healthy communities in rural and urban America. The Foundation identifies programs that focus on workforce training, weatherization, utility bill payment assistance, renewable energy education and access to clean air, water and healthy food. Through its college internship program, Sharing the Power prepares the next generation of energy business and sustainability leaders for careers in the public and private sectors.

In 2020, Volt Energy Utility founder Gilbert Campbell envisioned a process through which the basic tool used to finance major solar projects, the power purchase agreement (PPA), could produce a funding stream to support environmental justice causes. A percentage of revenue generated by these projects would be dedicated to funding a foundation. Volt developed the Environmental Justice PPA, which later became the Community Impact PPA. In 2021, with the assistance and support of Microsoft, Sharing the Power Foundation became a reality. Since that time, corporate partners such as Google and others have also supported the Foundation.

We partner with environmental causes by funding grassroots solutions to complex environmental challenges, and we invest in the next generation of environmental leaders by equipping students with the experience, professional development, and funding they need to thrive in sustainability and clean energy careers. With the continued collaboration of the communities we serve, and the guidance and generous contributions of our corporate partners, Sharing the Power Foundation continues to be a leader in providing essential financial and programmatic support to community-based organizations nationwide.

Workforce & Economic Development

Building the talent pipeline from community to clean energy career.

We fund training that puts people to work in the clean energy economy, including coalfield retraining, weatherization and efficiency crews, and solar installation, in cities, rural counties and remote communities alike. Energy Affordability

Cutting the household energy burden with dollars that reach the meter.

Many families spend 20–30% of their income on energy, and one in four skip bills to get by. Federal dollars cover part of the bill. We fund the permanent upgrades and the repairs federal programs will not pay for, at no cost to the household.

The first Community Impact PPA

No precedent and no template. Microsoft became the first off-taker to dedicate PPA revenue to community benefit, with the 110 MW Hickory Solar project built with Volt Energy Utility funding workforce, STEM and convening programs.

Google Community Investment Funds

No Community Impact PPA and no defined project. Google trusted the Foundation to run its entire community investment mandate across three data center states, from vetting partners to disbursement and reporting.

The next wave of infrastructure

From the energy transition to data centers

Data centers are essential, and often unpopular and misunderstood. They face the same consent bottleneck as energy projects, with higher stakes. The model transfers intact: neutral convening and trust-building, independent 501(c)(3) fund management, and community benefit written into the deal with OKRs and audit-ready reporting.
